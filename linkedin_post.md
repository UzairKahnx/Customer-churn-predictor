I just finished an end-to-end machine learning project — and it's live. 🚀

A telecom company was losing customers every month. Using the Telco Customer Churn dataset (7,043 customers), I set out to answer three questions: **why do they leave, who will leave next, and what should the retention team do about it?**

**What the data said:**
📉 43% of month-to-month customers churn — vs just 3% on two-year contracts
📅 Over half of all churn happens in the first 6 months
💳 Electronic-check users churn at 45%, vs 17% on automatic bank payment
🌐 Fibre customers churn at 42%, vs 19% on DSL

**What I built:**
✅ A cleaned, explored dataset with a full 11-step Colab notebook
✅ Three models compared (Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest), with and without balanced class weights
✅ A tuned model with **78% recall** — because missing a churner costs more than an extra retention offer
✅ A live Streamlit app where you can change a customer's details, see their risk level, explore the model's drivers, and run "what-if" scenarios

The hardest part wasn't the modelling — it was choosing the right metric. A model with 80% accuracy looked great until I realised it was missing almost half the people who actually left. Optimising for recall changed the whole result.
